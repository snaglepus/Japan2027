The plan at a glance

One clean westward arc, then home through Tokyo

After a night in Tokyo we head into the mountains: three days skiing at Nozawa Onsen, two food-and-culture cities (Kanazawa and Takayama) with the snow village of Shirakawa-go on the way, a couple of days in Kyoto, two days of prestige onsen beneath Mt Fuji, then a relaxed Tokyo finish. No Osaka, round-trip Haneda.

Stop 01 · Arrival

Tokyo, a soft landing

Senso-ji, Asakusa, under winter snow
1 night · arrival

Ease into Japan

From Haneda · 30 to 45 min to the city
See & do

Land late afternoon and keep it easy: the lantern-lit lanes of Senso-ji and Asakusa, or simply dinner near the hotel. The big sights are saved for the finish.

Stay (near Tokyo Station for the morning train)

The Tokyo Station Hotel (splurge, inside the 1915 station), Hotel Metropolitan Marunouchi (mid-range, covered access), or Mitsui Garden Hotel Kyobashi (value).

Curious?

If you arrive near 3 February, the Setsubun bean-throwing ceremonies at Senso-ji fling roasted soybeans to chase out demons, sometimes with sumo wrestlers joining in.

Stop 02 · The snow

Nozawa Onsen

Nozawa Onsen village under snow, Mt Kenashi behind
3 nights · ski + onsen town

Ski days, hot-spring nights

From Tokyo · ~2h (shinkansen to Iiyama + bus)
Do

Ski a mountain that suits mixed abilities (the long, gentle Forest run for cruisers, steeper Challenge and Yamabiko powder for the keen), then soak in the village's 13 free public baths. A day trip to the Jigokudani snow monkeys (about 50 minutes) is a February highlight.

See

The Edo-era streets, the landmark Oyu bathhouse, and Ogama, the scalding source where locals still boil their eggs and vegetables.

Stay

Three rooms at a traditional ryokan (Nakajimaya, Sumiyoshiya, Chitosekan), or a self-contained 5-bedroom lodge for all six by the Nagasaka gondola.

Eat

Century-old soba at Sobadokoro Daimon, edomae sushi at Tengu Sushi, the special fire-cooked night at Mont, plus Libushi craft beer.

Snow monkeys at Jigokudani, an easy day trip
Curious?

The public baths are owned and scrubbed by the residents themselves, organised by neighbourhood, a system that has run since the Edo period.

Stop 03 · Culture & seafood

Kanazawa

The Higashi Chaya teahouse district
2 nights · the little Kyoto of the north

Gold leaf and snow crab

From Nozawa · ~2.5h (via Nagano)
See

Kenroku-en, one of Japan's three great gardens (the winter rope cones are up in February), the adjoining castle, the Higashi Chaya geisha quarter, the 21st Century Museum, and the trick-door Ninja Temple (tour by reservation).

Eat (peak snow-crab and nodoguro season)

Sashimi bowls at Omicho Market, high-end sushi at Otome Sushi, refined Kaga kaiseki at Zeniya, warming oden bars, and the famous gold-leaf soft-serve at Hakuichi.

Stay

Kinjohro (the splurge ryokan near the garden), Hyatt Centric Kanazawa (modern, by the station), or Hotel Nikko.

Do

A gold-leaf workshop (apply leaf to chopsticks or a box), a tea ceremony in a Higashi Chaya teahouse, and sake tasting at Fukumitsuya.

Curious?

Kanazawa makes about 99% of all the gold leaf in Japan, which is why you can eat a whole sheet of it on an ice cream. The name means "marsh of gold".

Stop 04 · On the way

Shirakawa-go

The thatched gassho-zukuri village of Ogimachi
Daytime stop · free on the bus

A village made of prayer hands

On the Kanazawa to Takayama highway bus
Do (2 to 3 hours)

Walk the lanes of the UNESCO-listed thatched village under snow, step inside a farmhouse such as Wada House, then climb to the Shiroyama viewpoint for the classic snow-blanketed photo.

Worth knowing

The evening winter light-up runs only a handful of dates and needs a scarce village-stay or tour package booked months ahead. If you miss it, Takayama's lit folk village is the snowy-night substitute.

Curious?

The steep "praying hands" roofs are built without a single nail and re-thatched communally under a mutual-aid system called yui, the whole village redoing one roof in a day.

Stop 05 · Hida old town

Takayama

Sanmachi Suji, the preserved Edo merchant streets
2 nights · sake, Hida beef, snow

Little Kyoto of the mountains

From Shirakawa-go · ~50 min by bus
See

The wooden machiya of Sanmachi Suji, the riverside Miyagawa morning market, Takayama Jinya (the last Edo-era government house), and the lantern-lit thatched farmhouses of Hida no Sato.

Eat (Hida beef A5 wagyu)

Charcoal yakiniku at Hidagyu Maruaki, riverside sukiyaki at Nakabashi Wakachiya, hoba miso at Suzuya, plus Hida beef skewers and beef-on-a-cracker from the street stalls.

Stay

Honjin Hiranoya Kachoan (benchmark onsen ryokan, only 18 rooms), the friendly in-town Oyado Koto no Yume, or alpine onsen at Hodakaso Yamano Hotel.

Do

A sake-brewery crawl is the signature: six or seven 100-year-old breweries inside the old town pour tasting flights, and winter is peak brewing season.

Hida no Sato open-air folk village
Curious?

Look for the sugidama, a ball of cedar boughs above a brewery door. Fresh green means this season's sake has just been pressed; as it browns, so does the sake.

Stop 06 · The old capital

Kyoto

The thousand torii of Fushimi Inari
2 nights · a winter return

Temples without the crowds

From Takayama · ~3 to 3h45 (via Nagoya)
See

Classics that reward a winter visit: Fushimi Inari at first light, Kinkaku-ji with a dusting of snow, Arashiyama, and the Gion lanes. Quieter picks: the Zen sub-temples of Daitoku-ji and Kitano Tenmangu's plum garden.

Eat

Benchmark kaiseki at Kikunoi, an intimate kappo like Gion Matayoshi, tofu hot pot at Okutan Nanzenji, convivial wagyu at Yazawa, and grazing at Nishiki Market.

Stay

A sleeps-six machiya townhouse so the group is under one roof, a ryokan splurge at Seikoro, or the reliable Hyatt Regency.

Do

A private tea ceremony, the Fushimi sake district in fresh-sake season, a guided Gion evening, and a day trip to Nara.

Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion, under snow
Curious?

Each vermilion torii at Fushimi Inari is donated by a business, with the company name painted on the back, so the climb is a stroll through centuries of corporate prayers for good fortune.

Stop 07 · The prestige onsen

Lake Kawaguchi & Mt Fuji

Chureito Pagoda framed by Mt Fuji
2 nights · the wind-down

Two days beneath the mountain

From Kyoto · ~3.5 to 4h (shinkansen to Mishima + transfer)
Stay

Gora Kadan Fuji, a new luxury ryokan built to frame Mt Fuji, with private onsen in the suites and in-house kaiseki. The deliberate slow finish before Tokyo.

See (winter is the clearest Fuji of the year)

Go early for the mirror reflection at Oishi Park, the red Chureito Pagoda, the Kachi Kachi ropeway, and a lake cruise.

Eat (local dish: hoto)

Hearty miso-and-pumpkin hoto noodles at Hoto Fudo or Hotokura Funari (with a Fuji view), and charcoal-hearth grilling at Sanrokuen.

Do

Fuji-view cafes around the north shore, the retro Honcho-dori streetscape in Fujiyoshida, and short winter queues for the coasters at Fuji-Q Highland.

Curious?

The upside-down Fuji reflected on a still lake is called Sakasa Fuji, and the view from this very lake appears on the back of the 1,000 yen note.

Stop 08 · The finish

Tokyo, the big finish

The Shibuya Scramble Crossing
2 nights · food, lights, fun

Everything you saved for last

From Kawaguchi · ~2h by limited express
See

Shibuya Sky at sunset (Mt Fuji on clear days), the Scramble Crossing, Meiji Jingu, and the winter illuminations around Marunouchi.

Eat

Three-star kaiseki at Ryugin, lively izakaya Narukiyo, yakitori with great sake at Jomon, the charcoal alley of Omoide Yokocho, and a morning at Tsukiji Outer Market.

Do

Pick one teamLab, a street go-kart tour (each driver needs an International Driving Permit), the tiny bars of Golden Gai, and an easy day trip to Kawagoe (Little Edo) or Kamakura.

Curious?

Omoide Yokocho's affectionate old nickname is "Piss Alley", a warren of postwar yakitori stalls still going strong under Shinjuku's skyscrapers.

Worth booking early

What to lock in first

February is peak for snow and onsen, and a few of these are small and fill months ahead. Rough order of priority once the dates are firm:

  1. Gora Kadan Fuji (three rooms) and the Nozawa lodging, the two most constrained.
  2. Any Shirakawa-go winter light-up package, if wanted (very limited dates and access).
  3. Top tables: Kikunoi (Kyoto), Ryugin (Tokyo), Otome or Zeniya (Kanazawa), and the Takayama ryokan.
  4. The Nohi bus seats (Kanazawa to Takayama), the Ninja Temple tour, the gold-leaf workshop, and teamLab slots.
  5. The snow-monkey tour from Nozawa, and group dinners in Takayama and Nozawa.
